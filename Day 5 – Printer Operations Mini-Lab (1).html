Ticket T1 – New Shared MFP Deployment
Configure drivers, connectivity, security, and scan settings for a new multifunction printer.
Workflow: 1) Pick a ticket. 2) Use the controls to actually change settings or maintenance actions. 3) Click the purple Simulate button for that ticket to see “logs”. 4) Hit Evaluate My Tickets when you’re done. Nothing is drag-and-drop – everything is hands-on configuration.
Scenario – Deploy a shared multifunction printer

Your company just purchased a new multifunction printer (print/scan/copy). It will sit in a shared office area and must support:

• Users on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
• Printing complex reports with charts and graphics.
• Centralized control over print queues and permissions.
• Secure printing so confidential jobs only release when the user is at the device.
• Scan-to-folder for a shared network folder called \\SERVER\Scans.

Use the controls to actually configure how this printer is deployed to the network.
Driver Type
Mixed OS and complex graphics? Think about a driver language that handles images well.
Connectivity for Shared Access
The goal is multiple users printing without being cabled directly to the device.
Centralize queues, permissions, and logging.
Protect confidential print jobs at the shared printer.
Scan Workflow
Users want scanned documents to land in a shared network folder automatically.
After setting the driver, connectivity, security, and scan options, click to see a simulated deployment summary for this printer.
T1 Scoring: 4 points – driver, network, security, and scan settings. Think: PostScript, Ethernet or print server, user auth, Scan-to-SMB.
Scenario – Faded, speckled laser printouts

A laser printer on the second floor has the following symptoms:

• Text and images look very light and sometimes uneven across the page.
• There are occasional speckles of toner on otherwise blank areas.
• The printer has printed tens of thousands of pages but recently started having this issue.

Use the maintenance panel to perform the first actions you would actually take on this printer.
Maintenance Actions (check everything you will perform)
Faded or uneven print on laser devices often starts here.
Typically based on page count – used for worn fuser/rollers.
Helps with speckles and dirty components.
This is specific to inkjet printers, not lasers.
Choose which maintenance you’ll perform, then click to simulate a test page after the work.
T2 Scoring: 3 points – correct first action and supporting maintenance. Day 5: replace toner first, then consider maintenance kit & cleaning.
Scenario – Inkjet feeding multiple sheets

An inkjet in the accounting department is having trouble with paper handling:

• Frequently pulls in two sheets at once.
• Sometimes misaligns pages, causing crooked prints.
• Users complain about repeated jams in the input tray.

Use the maintenance controls to pick your first hands-on step.
Maintenance Actions (for this inkjet)
Roller problems commonly cause multi-sheet feeds and misalignment.
Impact ribbons belong to dot matrix/impact printers.
Thermal maintenance is for POS / receipt printers.
Used more for streaks/missing lines than for multi-sheet feeding.
Perform your chosen maintenance, then run a simulated paper feed test to see if jams improve.
T3 Scoring: 3 points – rollers first, with correct printer type awareness. Day 5: clean rollers and feeder to fix multi-sheet issues.
Scenario – Network printer offline & jobs stuck

A user reports that their jobs never print:

• The printer is a network MFP listed by hostname.
• Print jobs show as “printing” but never leave the queue.
• Sometimes the printer shows as “offline” in Windows, even though it’s powered on.

Use the controls to choose your first troubleshooting actions.
Initial Troubleshooting Step
If jobs never reach the printer, think about connectivity and communication first.
Queue / Service Action
Stuck jobs often require clearing the print queue or restarting the spooler.
Prevents printers “moving” on the network unexpectedly.
Choose your network and queue actions, then simulate resending print jobs to see if they complete.
T4 Scoring: 4 points – network first, then spooler/queue management. Day 5: check network connection & spooler when jobs are stuck.
